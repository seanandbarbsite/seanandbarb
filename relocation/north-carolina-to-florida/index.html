The Short Answer

What Changes When You Move From North Carolina to Florida

When you make Florida your home, your North Carolina income tax, a flat rate of about 3.99% that is scheduled to keep declining, goes to zero. North Carolina is already a relatively low-tax state, so the move is less about escaping high taxes and more about eliminating state income tax entirely while gaining Florida's lifestyle. Neither state has an estate or inheritance tax. Property taxes are slightly lower in North Carolina, but Florida adds the Homestead Exemption and the Save Our Homes 3% cap. Below, compare the numbers, calculate your own savings, see where North Carolina buyers are landing in Central Florida, and learn exactly how to establish Florida residency.

Make It Official

How to Establish Florida Residency From North Carolina

Ending North Carolina income tax is not automatic; you have to change your domicile and document it. Because high-tax states can review a departing resident, a clean break matters. Here are the six steps:

1

Spend more than 183 days a year in Florida

Make Florida your primary home and keep simple records (a calendar, travel, card and phone data) showing where you actually spend your time.

2

File a Declaration of Domicile

Record a Declaration of Domicile with your Florida county clerk of court to formally state Florida is your permanent home.

3

Get a Florida driver license and register your vehicles

Obtain a Florida license, register your cars in Florida, and surrender your North Carolina license.

4

Register to vote in Florida

Register in your Florida county and cancel your North Carolina voter registration.

5

Move your financial and personal life

Update the IRS, banks, brokerages, employer, and estate documents to your Florida address, and shift doctors, memberships, and primary accounts here.

6

File a final North Carolina return and sever ties

File your last North Carolina resident or part-year return and, ideally, sell or lease the North Carolina home. Keep your documentation organized in case of a residency review.

This is general information, not legal or tax advice. Work with a Florida CPA and, for higher incomes, a residency attorney; Sean & Barb can introduce you to trusted professionals.

Your Questions, Answered

How much money do you save moving from North Carolina to Florida?

Florida has no state income tax, so the savings equal the North Carolina tax you stop paying. A household with $350,000 of taxable income saves roughly $13,965 a year. North Carolina is already a relatively low-tax state, so the move is less about escaping high taxes and more about eliminating state income tax entirely while gaining Florida's lifestyle. Use the calculator above to see your exact number, then call (407) 383-0707.

Does Florida have a state income tax?

No. Florida has no state income tax, no tax on capital gains, no estate tax, and no inheritance tax. Wages, bonuses, investment income, pensions, and retirement withdrawals are all free of state income tax once you are a Florida resident. North Carolina, by contrast, taxes income at a flat rate of about 3.99%.

How do North Carolina and Central Florida property taxes compare?

North Carolina's property taxes (~0.8%) are a bit lower than Central Florida's (~1.0% to 1.2%), but Florida has no state income tax and adds the Homestead Exemption and the Save Our Homes 3% cap, which protects you as values rise.

How do I establish Florida residency and domicile after moving from North Carolina?

Make Florida your primary home and document it: spend more than 183 days a year in Florida, file a Declaration of Domicile with your county clerk, get a Florida driver license, register your vehicles and to vote in Florida, and move your bank, doctors, estate documents, and memberships to your Florida address. Then file a final or part-year North Carolina return. This is general information, not legal or tax advice; confirm your plan with a CPA or attorney.

How many days do I have to live in Florida to avoid North Carolina income tax?

The practical target is to spend more than 183 days a year in Florida and make Florida your true domicile, then keep records (travel logs, card and phone data, a Declaration of Domicile). Day counting alone is not enough; what matters is where your primary home and life are centered. Once Florida is genuinely your domicile, North Carolina no longer taxes your income.

Will North Carolina tax me after I move to Florida?

Once you establish Florida as your domicile and are no longer a North Carolina resident, North Carolina does not tax your income. A clean break helps: a Declaration of Domicile, Florida licenses, time records, and selling or leasing the North Carolina home. Income actually earned from North Carolina sources can still be taxable, so confirm specifics with a CPA.

North Carolina buyers gravitate to Winter Park (Park Avenue walkability and the Chain of Lakes), Lake Nona (new construction and a master-planned, 15-minute-city feel), Windermere (Butler Chain lakefront and Isleworth), Dr. Phillips (Restaurant Row and gated luxury), Lake Mary (top Seminole County schools and gated Heathrow), and Winter Garden and Horizon West (newer luxury at strong value). How much more home can I buy in Central Florida than in North Carolina?

Comparable to slightly more for the money, with the bonus of no state income tax. Luxury homes in Winter Park, Windermere, Dr. Phillips, and Lake Nona typically run $800,000 to $3M, with lakefront estates above that.

Does Florida tax retirement income, Social Security, or pensions?

No. Florida does not tax Social Security, pensions, IRA or 401(k) withdrawals, or any retirement income at the state level, because it has no state income tax at all. North Carolina taxes retirement income at its flat rate (it exempts Social Security); Florida taxes none of it. For retirees, the Florida difference compounds every year.

Is it cheaper to live in Central Florida than in North Carolina?

It is close. North Carolina is already fairly affordable, so the decisive Florida advantage is zero state income tax plus the coast and theme-park access. Property taxes are slightly higher in Florida but capped by Save Our Homes. Sean and Barb can compare your numbers.

What property tax breaks do Florida homeowners get?

Once a Florida home is your primary residence you can file for the Homestead Exemption, which removes $50,000 from the assessed value, and the Save Our Homes cap, which limits annual assessment increases to 3% no matter how fast the market rises. Accumulated Save Our Homes savings are also portable to your next Florida homestead.
