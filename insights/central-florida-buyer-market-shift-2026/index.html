More Inventory, Longer Days on Market: Why Central Florida Buyers Have Room to Negotiate

Central Florida's market has rebalanced, and that is good news if you are buying. Orlando homes are now taking around 32 days to sell, up from about 22 a year ago, with price reductions appearing on roughly 22% of active listings. For luxury and physician buyers, that means more time to evaluate, inspect, and negotiate than the frenzied recent years allowed.

What the data shows

Across recent market reporting from sources including the Orlando Regional REALTOR Association, Redfin, and Zillow, the picture is consistent: inventory is higher than a year ago, days on market have lengthened, and sellers are more willing to offer concessions. The Orlando metro median sale price sits in the high $300,000s to around $400,000, reflecting modest year-over-year growth rather than the rapid appreciation of prior years. Meanwhile, the average Florida 30-year fixed mortgage rate has hovered around the mid-6% range, per Freddie Mac's weekly survey.

What it means for luxury and physician buyers

The headline is leverage. Where buyers recently competed against multiple same-weekend offers, many now get a second showing, an inspection contingency, and a real negotiation. Sellers offering closing-cost contributions and price reductions partially offset higher rates for prepared, pre-approved buyers.

The rate-versus-price logic

Waiting on the sidelines for rates to fall carries a real cost. The rate you take today is refinanceable; the price you pay is locked in. For buyers planning to stay five or more years, today's negotiating room can matter more than a fractional rate move. Our buying guide walks through how to approach an offer in a balanced market.

Frequently asked questions

Is it a buyer's market in Orlando right now? It has rebalanced toward buyers with more inventory, longer days on market, and more price reductions, though it is not a full buyer's market.

Should I wait for mortgage rates to drop before buying? Waiting carries real costs; rates are refinanceable, but purchase price is fixed. Negotiating leverage today can outweigh a fractional rate change.
