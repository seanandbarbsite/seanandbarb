Where Physicians Practice in Central Florida: A Guide to the Major Hospital Systems

Central Florida's medical landscape has transformed over the past five years into one of the most dynamic in the Southeast, anchored by AdventHealth, Orlando Health, and the Lake Nona Medical City ecosystem. For a relocating physician, where you practice shapes where you should live. Here is a tour of the major systems across the markets we serve, with the details that matter to your career and your commute.

AdventHealth Orlando

The flagship of the region. AdventHealth Orlando is home to the only Transplant Institute in Orlando, offering robot-assisted kidney transplants, alongside the GRACE genomic cancer risk program, a Level II NICU through the Little Miracles Unit, and the AdventHealth Cancer Institute. A $1 billion campus expansion, including a 14-story, 440-bed surgical tower with 24 operating rooms, was announced in May 2025 and is slated to open by 2030. The campus currently hosts 24 accredited residency and fellowship programs.

Key specialties: transplant, cardiovascular, oncology, neuroscience, behavioral health, endocrinology, digestive health, women's services, and orthopedics.

Orlando Health Orlando Regional Medical Center (ORMC)

An 808-bed tertiary facility and the only Level I Trauma Center in Central Florida for adults and children. ORMC is one of the region's most sought-after destinations for trauma surgeons, neurosurgeons, and critical care specialists, with on-site trauma critical care surgeons, around-the-clock operating rooms, and comprehensive subspecialty availability. It draws patients from across the Southeastern United States, the Caribbean, and Latin America.

Key specialties: Level I trauma, cardiovascular, stroke, neuroscience, orthopedics, burn center, cancer, and bariatrics.

AdventHealth Winter Garden

A full-service community hospital in the heart of West Orange County, AdventHealth Winter Garden operates 24/7 with emergency care and coordinated pediatric ER services, plus a multi-specialty outpatient clinic. For physicians who want a community-hospital setting with quick access to the flagship, it pairs naturally with living in Winter Garden or Windermere.

Lake Nona Medical City

Lake Nona has become a magnet for physicians and researchers, anchored by the UCF College of Medicine ecosystem and a growing cluster of clinical and research institutions. The greater Orlando metro now exceeds 3.5 million residents and continues to grow faster than nearly every major U.S. metro, which means physician demand keeps rising.
