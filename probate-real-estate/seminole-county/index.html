Can You Sell a House During Probate in Seminole County?

Yes. Once the Eighteenth Judicial Circuit appoints a personal representative and the clerk issues letters of administration, estate property in Seminole County can be listed and sold during administration rather than after it. If the will grants a specific power of sale, the personal representative may sell without a separate court order (Fla. Stat. 733.613). Protected homestead is the exception that matters most: the decedent's primary residence typically passes straight to the heirs, who sign the listing and sell it themselves. The process is state law. What changes in Seminole County is where you file, what it costs, and how the local docket moves.

Seminole County Probate Is Not Filed at the Courthouse Most People Drive To

Probate filings in Seminole County go through the Seminole County Clerk of the Circuit Court & Comptroller, and here is the detail that trips up families every week: the clerk's probate office is not at the Civil Courthouse on Park Avenue. It sits at the Juvenile Justice Center, 190 Eslinger Way, Sanford, FL 32773, reachable at 407-665-4369. That is where estate filings, certified copies, and status questions are handled for every family in Lake Mary, Longwood, Oviedo, Winter Springs, Casselberry, Altamonte Springs, Sanford, and unincorporated Seminole County.

In practice you may never need the address. Attorneys file electronically through the Florida Courts E-Filing Portal, the Eighteenth Judicial Circuit requires a completed checklist to accompany probate filings so files reach the judge clean, and many routine, uncontested matters are handled without a hearing when the paperwork is in order. Florida law still requires the person holding the original will to deposit it with the clerk within 10 days of learning of the death (Fla. Stat. 732.901). In Seminole County that deposit is free, with a $7 charge only if you want a receipt.

What Probate Costs to Open in Seminole County

Clerk filing fees currently run approximately $400 for formal administration and approximately $345 for summary administration, with publication of the notice to creditors adding roughly $150 to $300 depending on the newspaper. Fees change, so confirm the current schedule with the Seminole County Clerk of the Circuit Court & Comptroller before filing. Attorney fees are separate and are typically the larger number; Florida sets presumptively reasonable rates by estate value, and your probate attorney will quote them up front.

Worth knowing before any of that: the estate's largest cost is usually not a fee at all. It is the carrying cost of a vacant house, often $2,000 to $3,500 a month for a typical Central Florida estate home, plus the vacant-home insurance restrictions that can void coverage after 30 to 60 days. Our net-to-estate calculator puts numbers on what waiting costs.

Florida Doubled the Summary Administration Limit

Effective July 1, 2026, Florida raised the summary administration threshold under Fla. Stat. 735.201 from $75,000 to $150,000 in non-exempt probate assets. The change applies to decedents who pass away on or after July 1, 2026, and it means more Seminole County estates qualify for the faster track that skips the appointment of a personal representative and resolves by court order. Homestead, retirement accounts, life insurance, and other non-probate assets do not count toward the cap, so an estate can hold a valuable Lake Mary home and still qualify when the home is protected homestead. For deaths before July 2026, the prior $75,000 limit may still control. Which track applies is a legal call your probate attorney makes; what we contribute is the documented property value that petition needs.
